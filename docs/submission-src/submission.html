NeeruAI reduces a data centre's freshwater demand using closed liquid cooling, recovers water from its concentrated reject stream using the facility's own waste heat, and cryptographically verifies every recovered litre before it is reused or released.

India's data centres consume roughly 150 billion litres of water a year, much of it drawn from aquifers already past their sustainable limit. No regulation requires a facility to publish what it actually withdraws or discharges, and no agency verifies that the water source named in a clearance matches what happens on site. That unverifiable gap is the problem this project addresses — and MongoDB is the reason the solution is buildable as a single coherent data layer rather than four separate systems stitched together.

Working prototype: six-page operations console with a live physical simulation of the plant. Contact: jyothirrr.444@gmail.com

01Executive summary

NeeruAI is a software layer — not a treatment plant. It makes water-recovery hardware that a data centre already owns, or could buy from any vendor, coordinated, forecastable and independently auditable.

The three layers

LayerWhat it doesMechanism
01 REDUCECuts the freshwater draw at its sourceClosed-loop liquid cooling — direct-to-chip or immersion — instead of open evaporative towers. The coolant recirculates; it is not consumed.
02 RECOVERTakes back what is still lostWaste heat drives membrane distillation on the blowdown / RO-reject stream: already concentrated, already contaminated, today simply drained.
03 VERIFYProves it happenedInline probes → automated quality gate against BIS IS 10500 drinking-water limits → hash-chained, independently recomputable ledger.

Why this is a software problem

Recoverable heat is a function of IT load, and IT load in India's mixed air- and liquid-cooled fleet is volatile and site-specific. A fixed hardware topology cannot absorb that variance. A forecasting and dispatch layer can — and it works with whatever treatment asset a facility already runs, whether that is a reverse-osmosis skid, an existing effluent treatment plant, a municipal ozonation unit, or a future membrane distillation module.

The nearest prior art, a 2018 patent covering waste-heat-driven low-temperature desalination, claims a hardware method on one steady liquid-cooled loop with a seawater feed. It claims nothing about monitoring, forecasting, dispatch or verification, and its assumptions do not survive contact with an inland Indian facility whose water source is undisclosed and whose heat availability swings with a training run.

What was built

  • A six-page operations console that simulates the entire plant from one physics chain: IT load → heat → coolant temperature → membrane flux → permeate chemistry → pass/fail → dispatch → statutory report. Changing any control on one page moves every downstream page consistently, because there is exactly one model underneath.
  • A pore-scale membrane distillation simulation in which water vapour crosses hydrophobic pores while fluoride, nitrate and dissolved-salt particles are rejected — with flux computed from the Antoine vapour-pressure relation and corrected for temperature polarisation.
  • A hash-chained verification ledger with a working independent audit, plus a fault-injection control that breaches the membrane and demonstrates the quality gate quarantining a non-compliant batch in real time.
  • A MongoDB data architecture covering ingestion, forecasting, reactive dispatch, geospatial prioritisation, compliance reporting and ledger integrity — detailed in §05, which is the substance of this submission.
The MongoDB thesis in one paragraph

This system is four workloads that normally live in four different databases: a high-frequency IoT stream, a polymorphic device-integration layer, a geospatial routing problem, and an append-only audit log with a regulatory reporting API on top. MongoDB collapses all four into one deployment — time-series collections for the stream, flexible documents for the polymorphism, 2dsphere for the routing, and a hash-chained document field for the audit log, with change streams tying them together reactively and the aggregation framework producing the regulator's report. Removing any one of those capabilities does not degrade the product; it removes a specific feature.

The water being drawn is not merely scarce; it is contaminated. Fluoride is geogenic, released from deep hard-rock aquifers, and causes skeletal fluorosis. Nitrate arrives from agricultural and sewage infiltration and exceeds the 45 mg/L limit across a large share of shallow Bengaluru wells. Both are non-volatile — which, as §3.3 explains, is precisely why the treatment technology chosen here works.

2.2 The seawater question is a false binary

Public debate frames this as "seawater cooling versus groundwater". That describes a choice that does not yet exist. Seawater cooling in India is proposed, not deployed: Andhra Pradesh's data centre policy floats it for the Tarluvada hyperscale park, but the environmental clearance for that project names no water source at all, and monitoring groups cannot establish whether it will draw desalinated, surface or ground water.

The real, present contest is between groundwater, municipal supply, treated non-potable water, and recycled blowdown — and almost no operator transparently discloses which they use:

  • Nxtra by Airtel's 2025 ESG report: 216,357 KL total consumption — 9,876 KL groundwater, 191,152 KL purchased third-party, zero surface water.
  • A Greater Noida operator in a notified over-exploited zone claims it uses neither groundwater nor municipal drinking water, while officials would not confirm whether it holds extraction permission at all.
  • A second Greater Noida facility runs entirely on the industrial authority's treated non-potable network — the correct model, and one worth evidencing publicly so others copy it.

Nor is seawater automatically benign: thermal discharge, intake entrainment, seabed disturbance from pipe runs, biofouling chemistry and desalination brine are all live impacts. The defensible position is that seawater can reduce freshwater demand, but moving the cooling problem from an aquifer to the ocean does not by itself create a closed loop.

2.3 The regulatory gap that defines the product

Groundwater extraction requires a No Objection Certificate specifying a withdrawal cap. Beyond that:

  • Companies are not required to publish actual consumption or discharge figures. The EU mandates disclosure; India does not.
  • Once a clearance is granted, no agency verifies that the declared water source matches on-site practice.
  • Karnataka's 2022 Data Centre Policy required no water sustainability report whatsoever.

The consequence is that corporate "water-neutral" claims are unfalsifiable. Without independent verification they are indistinguishable from greenwashing — and that is the hole a verification layer fills.

2.4 What actually happens to cooling water

Cooling water splits into two fates, and the distinction matters because operators can report against the more flattering one.

  • Evaporative loss — true consumption. In an evaporative tower, water absorbs heat and partially evaporates. That fraction is permanently removed from the local watershed. This is why withdrawal and consumption are different numbers.
  • Blowdown — the wastewater stream. As water cycles, dissolved solids concentrate until they would scale and corrode the plant, so a portion is drained. Blowdown is more concentrated than the intake — more fluoride, more nitrate, more TDS — plus biocides and corrosion inhibitors.

From there a facility has three options: discharge it under permit (the wasteful default), recycle it into the cooling loop (a Greater Noida effluent treatment plant achieved over 95% water savings doing exactly this), or eliminate discharge entirely through Zero Liquid Discharge at high capital cost. NeeruAI's recovery stage targets this stream specifically, because it is the wastewater India's data centres already produce today — independent of whether they ever adopt new cooling hardware.

03The solution — reduce, recover, verify

3.1 The physical chain

SERVERS ──► COLD PLATE ──► CDU / COOLANT LOOP ──► HEAT EXCHANGER ──┬──► COOLING TOWER (heat rejected) │ └──► MD FEED HEATER │ COOLING TOWER ──► BLOWDOWN TANK ──► MD SKID ◄─────────────────────────────────┘ │ ┌────────────┴────────────┐ ▼ ▼ QUALITY GATE ZLD CRYSTALLISER (BIS IS 10500) (concentrate) │ ┌───────────┴───────────┐ ▼ ▼ COOLING MAKEUP WARD DISPATCH (loop closed on site) (borewell recharge / taps)

3.2 Layer 1 — reduce, at the cold plate

A processor die rejects heat through a thermal interface into a cold plate: a metal block with parallel microchannels. Coolant enters cold, absorbs heat along the channel, leaves hot — and returns. It is a sealed recirculating loop, not a consumable.

Contrast with air cooling, which produces low-grade diffuse heat and, with evaporative assist, consumes water continuously. Liquid cooling captures heat at the source, at a higher and more concentrated temperature. That single architectural change does two things at once: it collapses freshwater makeup demand, and it upgrades the waste heat into something thermally useful. Layer 1 is therefore not an efficiency footnote — it is what makes Layer 2 physically possible, because air-cooled waste heat is too low-grade to drive distillation.

3.3 Layer 2 — recover, at the membrane

A hydrophobic membrane, typically PTFE with pores around 0.2 µm, separates two channels. Hot concentrated blowdown flows on one side; cool permeate on the other. Because the membrane is hydrophobic, liquid water cannot pass the pores — surface tension holds it out. Water vapour can. The temperature difference creates a vapour-pressure difference; water evaporates at the hot surface, crosses the pore, and condenses on the cold side.

Fluoride, nitrate and dissolved salts are non-volatile. They do not evaporate, so they physically cannot follow. Rejection here is a phase-change property, not a filtration efficiency being claimed.

Why membrane distillation and not reverse osmosis

Reverse osmosis is pressure-driven and runs on electricity — it cannot consume waste heat at all. Specifying RO would collapse the premise that the facility's own rejected heat is the energy input; the system would simply be buying more grid power. Membrane distillation is thermally driven, making it the only mainstream separation technology that actually consumes the byproduct being repurposed.

Temperature polarisation is the detail that separates a working design from a paper one: the membrane surface is never as hot as the bulk feed, because thermal boundary layers form on each side and absorb part of the driving force. The polarisation coefficient τ is the ratio between the ΔT the membrane actually sees and the ΔT an operator measures. Ignoring it overstates flux by roughly 30%, and the prototype models and displays it explicitly.

The concentrate is not hidden. Membrane distillation leaves behind a smaller but more concentrated brine than the blowdown it started from. The architecture names its disposal pathway — a ZLD crystalliser or licensed industrial reuse — because a recovery system that conceals its own residual stream is committing the greenwashing it claims to correct.

3.4 Layer 3 — verify

Inline probes (conductivity/TDS, fluoride ion-selective electrode, nitrate, pH, turbidity) log automatically rather than being self-reported. Every batch is gated against BIS IS 10500, the Indian drinking-water specification — deliberately stricter than the industrial-effluent limits a facility would otherwise be judged against. A batch failing any parameter is quarantined and re-treated; it is never released, and the failure remains on the record.

Each batch becomes a document storing the hash of the previous batch's document. Altering any historical record breaks every subsequent hash. This gives tamper-evidence with no blockchain infrastructure — a design that fits inside a hackathon and inside a regulator's audit procedure equally well.

3.5 The coastal variant

For coastal sites — Chennai, Mumbai, Visakhapatnam, Jamnagar — seawater never touches the servers. A closed secondary loop meets seawater only across a heat exchanger, with intake temperature, discharge temperature, ΔT and flow instrumented into the same ledger. The objective is a measured and bounded thermal footprint, not the pretence that none exists.

05MongoDB data architecture

This section is the substance of the submission. Each capability below is load-bearing: remove it and a named feature of the product stops working. Nothing here is used decoratively.

5.1 Why a document database at all

NeeruAI is four workloads that conventionally live in four different systems:

  1. A high-frequency sensor stream — thousands of readings per facility per minute, written far more than read, queried almost always by time range.
  2. A polymorphic device-integration layer — every treatment vendor emits a different reading shape, and the set of vendors grows with every customer.
  3. A geospatial routing problem — which village, ward or borewell should receive surplus water, ranked by published stress data and distance.
  4. An append-only audit log with a regulatory reporting API built on top of it.

A relational stack would typically answer this with a time-series database, plus a relational core, plus a PostGIS extension or separate geo service, plus an application-managed audit table — four operational surfaces, four backup strategies, and joins across a network boundary every time the dispatch engine needs to combine a live reading with a facility's stress polygon.

MongoDB collapses all four into one deployment with one query language and one operational surface. The following sub-sections show each one concretely.

5.3 Time-series collections — the ingestion floor

Telemetry is created as a native time-series collection rather than an ordinary one. MongoDB then stores readings in an internal bucketed, columnar format, which is materially better suited to this access pattern than row-per-reading storage: writes are append-heavy, reads are almost always "this facility, this device, this window", and the values within a bucket compress well because consecutive readings are similar.

// Telemetry is append-only, time-ordered, and queried by window.
// A native time-series collection gives bucketing, columnar storage and
// automatic expiry without any application-side partitioning logic.
db.createCollection("sensor_readings", {
  timeseries: {
    timeField:   "ts",
    metaField:   "source",        // {facility_id, device_id, device_type, vendor}
    granularity: "seconds"
  },
  expireAfterSeconds: 60 * 60 * 24 * 400   // 400-day hot window; older data → Online Archive
})

The metaField choice is deliberate. Everything that identifies where a reading came from and never changes within a stream goes into source; everything that varies reading to reading goes into metrics. That is what lets MongoDB group readings from one device into the same bucket and keep bucket counts low.

// One reading document as written by the ingestion layer
{
  ts: ISODate("2026-09-08T14:22:03.000Z"),
  source: {
    facility_id: "DC-DVN-03",
    device_id:   "CDU-01",
    device_type: "cdu",
    vendor:      "Vertiv XDU-450"
  },
  metrics: {
    coolant_supply_C: 32.0,
    coolant_return_C: 45.4,
    flow_L_min:       2184,
    it_load_pct:      74.2,
    heat_reject_kW:   1712,
    junction_temp_C:  78.6
  }
}

5.4 Polymorphic documents — what "hardware-agnostic" actually means

This is the single most important schema decision in the project, and the one that would be hardest to replicate in a relational store.

NeeruAI's commercial premise is that it plugs into whatever treatment hardware a facility already runs. But a Vertiv CDU, a domestic RO skid, a municipal ozonation plant and a research membrane distillation module emit completely different fields. A fluoride ion-selective electrode reports millivolts and a temperature-compensated concentration; a CDU reports flow, two temperatures and a pump duty; an ETP reports turbidity, BOD proxy and a dosing rate.

Under a rigid schema, onboarding the second vendor requires a migration; the tenth requires either forty mostly-null columns or an unqueryable JSON blob column. Under MongoDB, each device type writes its native shape into the same collection, and the reading is queryable on its own terms immediately:

// Three device types, one collection, no migration, all queryable

// Fluoride ion-selective electrode
{ ts: …, source: { device_type: "ise_probe", vendor: "Hanna HI4110", … },
  metrics: { electrode_mV: -142.6, temp_C: 27.9, fluoride_mgL: 0.41,
             slope_mV_decade: -57.2, last_cal: ISODate("2026-09-01") } }

// Reverse-osmosis skid
{ ts: …, source: { device_type: "ro_skid", vendor: "Ion Exchange INDION", … },
  metrics: { feed_pressure_bar: 14.2, permeate_L_h: 810, reject_L_h: 390,
             conductivity_feed_uS: 2740, conductivity_perm_uS: 41 } }

// Membrane distillation module
{ ts: …, source: { device_type: "md_skid", vendor: "pilot-DCMD-40", … },
  metrics: { feed_in_C: 52.4, feed_out_C: 46.1, perm_in_C: 28.0, perm_out_C: 33.7,
             flux_LMH: 18.4, module_area_m2: 40, tmp_kPa: 3.1 } }

Validation is applied to the envelope only — the fields every device must supply — leaving metrics open. This is the practical middle ground between "no schema" and "a schema that blocks the next integration":

db.runCommand({
  collMod: "sensor_readings",
  validator: { $jsonSchema: {
    bsonType: "object",
    required: ["ts", "source", "metrics"],
    properties: {
      ts:     { bsonType: "date" },
      source: { bsonType: "object",
                required: ["facility_id", "device_id", "device_type"],
                properties: {
                  facility_id: { bsonType: "string" },
                  device_type: { enum: ["cdu","tower","ro_skid","etp",
                                        "md_skid","ise_probe","flow_meter"] } } },
      metrics: { bsonType: "object" }   // intentionally open — this is the integration surface
    } } },
  validationLevel: "moderate"
})

5.5 Change streams — reactive dispatch instead of polling

The dispatch engine must respond to a falling coolant return temperature within seconds: below roughly 38 °C the vapour-pressure driving force collapses and continuing to run the MD skid wastes pump energy for negligible permeate. A polling loop either wastes queries or reacts late. A change stream makes the reading itself the trigger.

// The new reading PUSHES the scheduler. No cron, no polling interval to tune.
const pipeline = [
  { $match: {
      operationType: "insert",
      "fullDocument.source.device_type": { $in: ["cdu", "md_skid", "ise_probe"] }
  } },
  { $project: { "fullDocument.ts": 1, "fullDocument.source": 1, "fullDocument.metrics": 1 } }
];

const stream = db.collection("sensor_readings")
                  .watch(pipeline, { fullDocument: "updateLookup" });

for await (const change of stream) {
  const r = change.fullDocument;

  if (r.source.device_type === "cdu") {
    const feedC = r.metrics.coolant_return_C - HX_LOSS_C;
    await dispatch.evaluateSkid(r.source.facility_id, feedC);   // ramp / hold / idle
  }

  if (r.source.device_type === "ise_probe" && r.metrics.fluoride_mgL > BIS.F) {
    await dispatch.holdBatch(r.source.facility_id, {
      reason: `fluoride ${r.metrics.fluoride_mgL} mg/L exceeds BIS limit ${BIS.F}`
    });                                     // quarantine before a litre leaves the site
  }
}

Change streams are resumable: the consumer persists a resumeToken, so a restarted dispatch service continues from the last processed reading rather than replaying or skipping. For a system whose entire value proposition is a complete, gapless record, that guarantee is not a convenience — it is a correctness requirement.

The same mechanism drives community notification. An Atlas Trigger on treatment_batches fires a serverless function that sends an SMS or WhatsApp message to the ward office on each verified delivery — matching how information actually reaches a Devanahalli-type community, rather than assuming anyone there will open an enterprise dashboard.

5.6 Window functions — the forecast engine's feature pipeline

Forecasting recoverable heat needs rolling features: a moving average of rejected heat, its rate of change, and the same-hour value from previous days. These are computed inside the database with $setWindowFields, directly on the time-series collection, so the model service receives features rather than raw rows — which keeps a very large read off the network entirely.

db.sensor_readings.aggregate([
  { $match: {
      "source.facility_id": "DC-DVN-03",
      "source.device_type": "cdu",
      ts: { $gte: new Date(Date.now() - 7*24*3600*1000) }
  } },
  { $setWindowFields: {
      partitionBy: "$source.facility_id",
      sortBy: { ts: 1 },
      output: {
        heat_ma_30m: {
          $avg: "$metrics.heat_reject_kW",
          window: { range: [-1800, 0], unit: "second" } },      // smoothing
        heat_slope_kW_h: {
          $derivative: { input: "$metrics.heat_reject_kW", unit: "hour" },
          window: { documents: [-12, 0] } },                       // trend
        load_p95_6h: {
          $percentile: { input: "$metrics.it_load_pct", p: [0.95],
                         method: "approximate" },
          window: { range: [-21600, 0], unit: "second" } }        // burst headroom
      } } },
  { $project: { _id: 0, ts: 1, heat_ma_30m: 1, heat_slope_kW_h: 1, load_p95_6h: 1,
                heat_now: "$metrics.heat_reject_kW" } }
])

$derivative is doing real work here: the sign and magnitude of the heat slope is what tells the dispatch engine whether to pre-ramp the skid ahead of an incoming training burst or begin winding it down. In the console, this is the dashed forecast segment that visibly moves before the measured line does.

5.7 Geospatial dispatch — ranking who needs the water most

Surplus verified permeate is not sent to whoever asks first. It is ranked against CGWB block-level assessment data, weighted by stress and tempered by haulage distance, with a 2dsphere index on both facilities and destinations.

db.dispatch_targets.createIndex({ geo: "2dsphere" })

db.dispatch_targets.aggregate([
  { $geoNear: {
      near: facility.geo,                 // GeoJSON Point from the facilities collection
      distanceField: "dist_m",
      maxDistance: 12000,                 // 12 km tanker-viable radius
      query: { active: true, type: { $in: ["ward_tap", "recharge_shaft"] } },
      spherical: true
  } },
  { $addFields: {
      // stress dominates; distance only breaks ties between comparably stressed blocks
      priority: { $divide: [
        "$cgwb.stage_of_extraction_pct",
        { $add: [1, { $divide: ["$dist_m", 4000] }] }
      ] } } },
  { $sort: { priority: -1 } },
  { $limit: 3 }
])

Because facility geometry, destination geometry and the CGWB stress attribute live in the same database as the batch that is about to be dispatched, the entire decision — is this water clean, how much is spare, and who needs it most — resolves in one round trip. In a split stack that is a cross-service join on the hot path of every delivery.

5.8 Aggregation — the compliance API a regulator can consume

Karnataka's 2022 Data Centre Policy never required a water report. The Liquid Waste Management Rules, 2024 now impose a reuse floor of 20% by 2027–28 rising to 50% by 2030–31. NeeruAI generates that report from signed batches rather than operator declarations, as a single pipeline materialised with $merge:

db.treatment_batches.aggregate([
  { $match: { facility_id: facilityId, ts: { $gte: periodStart, $lt: periodEnd } } },

  { $group: {
      _id: { day: { $dateTrunc: { date: "$ts", unit: "day", timezone: "Asia/Kolkata" } } },
      reject_treated_L: { $sum: "$input_L" },
      recovered_L:      { $sum: { $cond: [ { $eq: ["$quality", "PASS"] }, "$output_L", 0 ] } },
      quarantined_L:    { $sum: { $cond: [ { $eq: ["$quality", "FAIL"] }, "$output_L", 0 ] } },
      to_makeup_L:      { $sum: { $cond: [ { $eq: ["$destination_type", "cooling_makeup"] },
                                          "$output_L", 0 ] } },
      to_community_L:   { $sum: { $cond: [ { $eq: ["$destination_type", "community"] },
                                          "$output_L", 0 ] } },
      heat_recovered_kWh: { $sum: "$heat_used_kWh" },
      batches:            { $sum: 1 },
      fluoride_max_mgL:   { $max: "$fluoride_mgL" },
      fluoride_p95_mgL:   { $percentile: { input: "$fluoride_mgL", p: [0.95],
                                           method: "approximate" } }
  } },

  { $lookup: { from: "facilities", localField: "facility_id",
               foreignField: "_id", as: "facility" } },
  { $set: { facility: { $first: "$facility" } } },

  { $set: {
      // the number the 2024 Rules actually ask for
      reuse_fraction_pct: { $multiply: [
        { $cond: [ { $gt: ["$reject_treated_L", 0] },
                   { $divide: ["$recovered_L", "$reject_treated_L"] }, 0 ] }, 100 ] },
      lwm_2024_status: { $switch: { branches: [
          { case: { $gte: ["$reuse_fraction_pct", 50] }, then: "MEETS_2030_31_TARGET" },
          { case: { $gte: ["$reuse_fraction_pct", 20] }, then: "MEETS_2027_28_TARGET" } ],
          default: "BELOW_FLOOR" } },
      noc_headroom_kld: { $subtract: ["$facility.noc_limit_kld",
                                      { $divide: ["$freshwater_withdrawn_L", 1000] } ] }
  } },

  { $merge: { into: "compliance_reports",
              on: ["facility_id", "_id.day"],
              whenMatched: "replace", whenNotMatched: "insert" } }
])

Because the output is materialised into its own collection, a regulator's dashboard reads pre-computed daily rows rather than re-scanning the ledger. The same pipeline runs unchanged at district or state scope by widening the $match and adding a grouping key — which is what makes this a plausible public instrument rather than a single operator's report.

5.9 The hash-chained ledger — tamper-evidence without a blockchain

The verification claim is only as good as the log's integrity. Each batch document stores the hash of its predecessor, so modifying any historical record invalidates every hash after it.

The subtlety is concurrency: two batches minted simultaneously must not both claim the same predecessor, or the chain forks. A multi-document transaction against a per-facility ledger_tips document serialises the chain head:

const session = client.startSession();
await session.withTransaction(async () => {
  // Atomically claim the next height and read the current head
  const tip = await tips.findOneAndUpdate(
    { _id: facilityId },
    { $inc: { height: 1 } },
    { returnDocument: "before", upsert: true, session }
  );

  const doc = {
    ...batch,                                   // litres, chemistry, verdict, destination
    facility_id: facilityId,
    height:    (tip?.height ?? 0) + 1,
    prev_hash:  tip?.hash ?? GENESIS
  };
  doc.hash = sha256(canonicalJSON(doc));        // deterministic field ordering

  await batches.insertOne(doc, { session });
  await tips.updateOne({ _id: facilityId },
                       { $set: { hash: doc.hash } }, { session });
});

Append-only behaviour is enforced at two levels rather than trusted to the application. A unique index on {facility_id, height} makes a fork impossible to persist, and the writing role is granted insert and find but not update or remove:

db.treatment_batches.createIndex({ facility_id: 1, height: 1 }, { unique: true })
db.treatment_batches.createIndex({ batch_id: 1 }, { unique: true })

db.createRole({
  role: "ledgerWriter",
  privileges: [ { resource: { db: "neeruai", collection: "treatment_batches" },
                  actions: ["insert", "find"] } ],   // no update, no remove
  roles: []
})

Verification is a single pass any third party can run — the console implements exactly this, and the audit button in the prototype executes it live:

db.treatment_batches.aggregate([
  { $match: { facility_id: facilityId } },
  { $sort:  { height: 1 } },
  { $setWindowFields: {
      partitionBy: "$facility_id", sortBy: { height: 1 },
      output: { expected_prev: { $shift: { output: "$hash", by: -1,
                                           default: GENESIS } } } } },
  { $match: { $expr: { $ne: ["$prev_hash", "$expected_prev"] } } }   // empty result = chain intact
])
Why this beats an actual blockchain here

The property a regulator needs is tamper-evidence, not decentralised consensus — there is no adversarial multi-party write problem to solve, because the operator is the only writer and the regulator is the auditor. A hash chain in a document field delivers the needed property with no consensus overhead, no token, no separate infrastructure to operate, and a verification procedure a state pollution control board's IT team can run in one query.

The shape that matters is the ratio: telemetry outnumbers ledger documents by roughly five orders of magnitude. Those two workloads have opposite requirements — telemetry is write-heavy, compressible, and expendable after its retention window; the ledger is tiny, immutable, and must be preserved indefinitely. Putting them in one deployment but different collection types lets each get the treatment it needs:

  • Telemetry — time-series collection with bucketing and columnar compression, a 400-day expireAfterSeconds hot window, and Atlas Online Archive beyond that for the cold tail a regulator might subpoena years later.
  • Ledger — a standard collection, never expired, with the unique index and restricted role from §5.9.
  • Reports — materialised daily rows, so the regulator-facing read path never touches the ledger scan.

What a real deployment must validate

  • Membrane rejection is feed- and membrane-specific. The model uses literature values; a deployment must measure actual rejection against its own blowdown chemistry rather than quoting a brochure figure.
  • Flux coefficient B and module area must be characterised for the specific membrane and module geometry procured.
  • Probe calibration drift — a fluoride ion-selective electrode needs scheduled recalibration, and the ledger should record calibration state alongside each reading so a batch's provenance includes the instrument's condition. The schema already carries last_cal on ISE readings for exactly this.
  • Concentrate disposal must be contracted before commissioning, not after.

09Sources & honest limitations

What is modelled, what is measured

Modelled from first principles and standards: the heat balance, loop ΔT, evaporation from latent heat, cycles of concentration, the Antoine vapour-pressure relation, temperature polarisation, the flux model, and the concentration-then-rejection chain producing permeate chemistry. The regulatory limits, extraction figures and reuse targets are as published. The hash chain is genuinely computed and genuinely re-verifiable.

Simulated: the telemetry itself is synthetic, generated by the model rather than read from a facility. The four facilities are composites assembled from published reporting, not live customers. Membrane coefficients are typical literature values, not measurements from a specific procured module. The prototype states this in its own footer.

The plant is simulated, but it is not faked — every number on screen is computed from a physical model, which is why a control changed on one page moves every other page consistently. What a real deployment requires is calibration, not redesign.

Closing

India is about to build a great deal of data centre capacity in places that cannot spare the water. The engineering to use less of it, and to recover most of what is still lost, largely exists. What does not exist is any mechanism to establish whether a facility is actually doing it. NeeruAI is that mechanism — and MongoDB is what makes it one system instead of four.
