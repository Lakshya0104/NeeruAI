The pitch in 30 seconds

If a judge asks "what is NeeruAI?"

NeeruAI is an AI-powered closed-loop cooling and water-recovery operating system for data centres. We reduce freshwater demand using closed liquid cooling, recover water from the concentrated reject stream using the facility's own waste heat, and verify every recovered litre before it is reused or released.

The tagline: don't just purify the water a data centre throws away — make the data centre need less water in the first place.

Three layers, and you should be able to name them in order without looking:

LayerWhat it doesHow
01 REDUCECuts the freshwater draw at its sourceClosed-loop liquid cooling — direct-to-chip or immersion — instead of open evaporative towers. The coolant recirculates; it is not consumed.
02 RECOVERTakes back what is still lostWaste heat drives membrane distillation on the blowdown / RO-reject stream: already concentrated, already contaminated, today simply drained.
03 VERIFYProves it happenedInline probes → AI quality gate → hash-chained public ledger, checked against BIS IS 10500 drinking-water limits, not industrial-effluent limits.

The one sentence that ties it together: heat availability tracks IT load, IT load is unpredictable, so a forecasting and dispatch layer is what makes any treatment hardware usable at all. That layer is the product. It is 100% software.

The argument in four moves

Deliver these in order. Each one closes off an objection before the judge can raise it.

Move 1 — The problem is proven, not hypothetical

Lead with numbers, because judges check whether you know yours.

  • 169% — Devanahalli's groundwater extraction as a share of its permissible limit. There is no headroom left.
  • 1,000 ft — typical borewell depth in that belt. Natural recharge only reaches about 200 ft. You are mining fossil water.
  • 60.47% — India's national stage of extraction (CGWB 2024); 11.1% of assessment units are over-exploited. 104.79% in Gautam Buddha Nagar shows this is a national pattern, not a Bengaluru story.
  • ~150 billion litres a year of water use by Indian data centres, on roughly 0.5% of national electricity (CEEW 2026).
  • Deep hard-rock aquifers in this geology carry fluoride above the WHO 1.5 mg/L limit, and shallow Bengaluru wells carry nitrate far above 45 mg/L. So the water being drawn is not just scarce, it is contaminated.

Move 2 — Kill the false binary before someone else does

Say this

"Seawater cooling in India is proposed, not deployed. Andhra Pradesh's policy floats it for the Tarluvada park, but the environmental clearance names no water source at all — activists monitoring it cannot establish whether it will be desalinated water, surface water or groundwater. So 'sea animals versus groundwater' describes a fight that isn't happening yet."

"The real fight today is groundwater versus municipal supply versus treated non-potable water versus recycled blowdown — and almost nobody discloses which they use."

Evidence to have ready if pushed: Nxtra by Airtel's 2025 ESG report shows 216,357 KL total, of which 9,876 KL groundwater and 191,152 KL purchased third-party, zero surface water. A Greater Noida operator in a notified over-exploited zone claims it uses neither groundwater nor municipal drinking water, while officials would not confirm whether it even holds extraction permission. Another Greater Noida facility runs entirely on the industrial authority's treated non-potable network.

And do not overclaim in the other direction. The defensible line is: seawater can reduce freshwater demand, but moving the cooling problem from an aquifer to the ocean does not by itself create a closed loop — there is still thermal discharge, intake entrainment, seabed disturbance, biofouling chemistry and brine.

Move 3 — Name the gap that is the product

A groundwater NOC fixes an extraction cap. Nothing requires a facility to publish actual consumption or discharge. Once a clearance is granted, no agency verifies that the declared water source matches what is happening on the ground. The EU mandates disclosure; India does not. Karnataka's 2022 Data Centre Policy required no water report at all.

Consequence: corporate "water-neutral" claims are unfalsifiable. That is the textbook definition of greenwashing risk — and it is exactly the hole a verification layer fills.

Move 4 — Why software, and why not the Oracle patent

Oracle's 2018 patent covers a hardware method: waste heat to low-temperature desalination, on one steady liquid-cooled loop, with a seawater feed and a fixed topology. It claims nothing about monitoring, forecasting, dispatch or verification.

India breaks every assumption in it — a mixed air- and liquid-cooled fleet, heat availability that tracks unpredictable IT load, inland and fragmented water sources, multiple regulators, and no disclosure regime. Just as Oracle needed MSET2 to make a fixed topology work, India needs a software layer to make any topology work.

The differentiation line

"Our opportunity isn't building better hardware than Oracle. It's being the operating system that makes any water-recovery hardware — an RO skid, an existing ETP, a BWSSB ozonation plant, a future MD unit — coordinated, verified and usable in India's messy, multi-agency water reality."

The hardware, explained

You need to be able to walk the water and the heat through the plant without hesitating. This is the whole physical chain in the order the console shows it.

1. The die and the cold plate

A GPU or CPU die rejects heat through a thermal interface material into a cold plate — a metal block with parallel microchannels machined into it. Coolant enters cold at one end, picks up heat as it travels, and leaves hot at the other. The critical point for your pitch: the same fluid comes back. It is a sealed recirculating loop, not a consumable.

Contrast with air cooling: hot air off the servers goes to a CRAC or an evaporative tower, which produces low-grade diffuse heat and, in the evaporative case, consumes water continuously. Liquid cooling captures heat at the source, at a higher and more concentrated temperature. That single architectural change does two things at once — it collapses freshwater makeup demand, and it upgrades the waste heat into something thermally useful.

Why this matters for the pitch

Layer 1 is not an afterthought — it is what makes Layer 2 physically possible. Air-cooled waste heat is too low-grade to drive distillation. Say this if someone asks why you insist on liquid cooling.

Immersion cooling — mention it, don't dwell

Immersion goes further: servers sit in an engineered dielectric fluid that absorbs heat directly from the electronics, then gives it up in a heat exchanger and returns. Same principle — recirculating, not consumed — with even better heat capture. Worth one sentence as the endpoint of the Layer 1 trajectory.

2. The CDU and the coolant loop

The Coolant Distribution Unit pumps the secondary loop through the cold plates and back. Two numbers define it: flow rate (litres per minute) and ΔT (how much the coolant heats up across the racks). More flow means lower ΔT and cooler silicon, but higher pump energy. Lower flow means a hotter return — which is better for heat recovery. That tension is real, and it is one of the things a dispatch engine gets to optimise.

3. The cooling tower — where the water actually goes

This is the part most people get wrong, so be precise. Cooling water splits into two separate fates:

  • Evaporative loss — true consumption. In an evaporative tower, water absorbs heat and partially evaporates. That fraction is permanently removed from the local watershed. This is why withdrawal and consumption are different numbers, and why a facility can honestly report low "usage" while still draining an aquifer.
  • Blowdown — the wastewater stream. As water cycles round the loop, dissolved solids concentrate. Past a certain point they would scale and corrode the equipment, so a portion has to be drained off. That drained portion is blowdown, and it is more concentrated than the intake — more fluoride, more nitrate, more TDS — plus biocides and corrosion inhibitors.

Cycles of concentration (CoC) is the ratio of dissolved solids in the circulating water to the makeup water. Run at 4 cycles and blowdown = evaporation ÷ 3. Run at 6.5 cycles and blowdown = evaporation ÷ 5.5 — less waste, but a more concentrated stream. The console shows CoC live and it changes with the architecture.

From there a facility has exactly three options, and this is the sentence that sets up your product:

  1. Discharge it with treatment and a permit — the wasteful default.
  2. Recycle it back into the cooling loop — a Greater Noida ETP achieved over 95% water savings doing this, and it is becoming a compliance requirement in Greater Noida, Pune and Hyderabad.
  3. Zero Liquid Discharge — eliminate discharge entirely, avoid permits, at high capex and opex.

4. The heat exchanger and the cascade

Instead of dumping all the return heat to the tower, a counter-flow heat exchanger taps it and hands it to the MD feed. Counter-flow means the two streams run in opposite directions, so the temperature difference stays roughly constant along the whole length rather than pinching at one end. The figure of merit is LMTD (log mean temperature difference) — it sets how hot you can get the feed, and feed temperature is what sets the flux downstream.

Heat cascading is the discipline of matching heat grade to task. Don't spend 90 °C heat on a job that 45 °C could do. Highest grade goes to distillation, the degraded remainder to preheating or absorption cooling, and only what is left after that goes to the tower.

5. The membrane distillation skid — the core

This is the piece to explain slowly.

A hydrophobic membrane (typically PTFE, ~0.2 µm pores) separates two channels. Hot concentrated blowdown flows on one side; cool permeate flows on the other. Because the membrane is hydrophobic, liquid water cannot pass through the pores — surface tension holds it out. But water vapour can.

The temperature difference creates a vapour-pressure difference across the membrane. Water evaporates at the hot surface, the vapour crosses the pore, and it condenses on the cold side. That is the entire mechanism.

Say this at the membrane

"Only water vapour crosses. Fluoride, nitrate and dissolved salts are non-volatile — they don't evaporate, so they physically cannot follow. Rejection here isn't a filtration efficiency we're claiming, it's a phase-change property."

Why MD and not RO — the question you will definitely get

Reverse osmosis is pressure-driven and runs on electricity. It cannot consume waste heat at all. If you used RO, the "we repurpose the facility's own rejected heat" premise collapses — you would just be buying more grid power. MD is thermally driven, so it is the only mainstream separation technology that actually eats the byproduct you claim to be repurposing.

Temperature polarisation — the detail that proves you're serious

The membrane surface is never as hot as the bulk feed. A thermal boundary layer forms on each side, so some of your measured ΔT is eaten before it reaches the membrane. The polarisation coefficient τ is the ratio of the ΔT the membrane actually sees to the ΔT you measure. Ignoring it is how paper designs overpromise flux by ~30%. The console draws this profile live.

The concentrate — never skip this

MD leaves behind a smaller, more concentrated brine than the blowdown it started with. Name the disposal pathway: a ZLD crystalliser, or licensed industrial reuse. A recovery pitch that hides its own residual stream is doing exactly the greenwashing it is attacking.

6. The quality gate and the ledger

Inline probes — conductivity/TDS, fluoride ion-selective electrode, nitrate, pH, turbidity — log automatically rather than being self-reported. Results are checked against BIS IS 10500, the Indian drinking-water specification, which is deliberately stricter than the industrial-effluent limits a facility would otherwise be judged against. Fail any parameter and the batch is quarantined and re-treated; it is never released, and the failure stays on the record.

Each passing or failing batch becomes a document that stores the hash of the previous batch's document. Change any historical record and every subsequent hash breaks. That gives you tamper-evidence without standing up blockchain infrastructure.

7. The coastal variant

For Chennai, Mumbai, Vizag or Jamnagar: seawater never touches the servers. A closed secondary loop meets seawater only across a heat exchanger, and intake temperature, discharge temperature, ΔT and flow are all instrumented into the same ledger. The goal is a measured, bounded thermal footprint — not the pretence that there isn't one.

What to point at

  • A coloured ion bouncing off the membrane — that is a rejection event. Say: "non-volatile, so it can't evaporate, so it can't follow."

The fault injection

Press Inject membrane wetting fault. Three pores turn red — liquid has intruded them, defeating the hydrophobic barrier. Now ions convect straight through instead of being left behind, drawn as red transits. Rejection collapses to ~85%, and fluoride is the first parameter to break the limit.

Say this during the fault

"This is the failure mode that matters, and it's why the gate checks against BIS IS 10500 drinking-water limits and not an industrial-effluent standard — an effluent standard would have waved this batch straight through."

Flux model

lower left

Flux against feed temperature, with the live operating point marked and crosshaired. The shaded band on the left is the region below 38 °C where the skid is idled.

The curve is the real model: J = B·(Pw(Tfm)·aw − Pw(Tpm)), with saturation vapour pressure from the Antoine equation and water activity reduced by the dissolved solids in the feed.

Say: "Flux rises exponentially with feed temperature, not linearly. That's why grade-matched waste heat is the right input, and it's the mathematical reason RO could never use this energy at all."

Five rows: the cooling makeup loop, three named community destinations with their stage of extraction, and the quarantine route. Each shows litres delivered today.

The ranking is a $geoNear query against CGWB block-level assessment polygons, re-scored on every change-stream event. Cooling makeup is served first — the loop closes before anything leaves the fence — and only surplus verified permeate is dispatched outward.

Say: "Priority is set by published groundwater stress data, not by whoever asks loudest. And ward offices get an SMS per delivery — not a dashboard login. That's how information actually reaches a Devanahalli-type community."

Statutory water balance

right

Ten rows: freshwater withdrawn, evaporative loss (consumed), blowdown generated, reject treated by MD, water recovered (verified), returned to cooling makeup, delivered to community, concentrate to ZLD, waste heat consumed, and discharged untreated: 0.

The footer computes the reuse fraction and chips it against the Liquid Waste Management Rules, 2024 — a mandatory floor of 20% by 2027–28 rising to 50% by 2030–31 for large water users.

Say: "This is rendered in the shape KSPCB or BWSSB would actually consume. Karnataka's 2022 Data Centre Policy required no water report at all. Every figure here traces back to a signed batch in the ledger — nothing is operator-declared."

Note the distinction between withdrawn and consumed on the first two rows. That distinction is the one most corporate reporting quietly blurs, and having it as two separate rows is itself a statement.

Judge Q&A

QuestionAnswer
Why not RO?RO is pressure-driven and runs on electricity — it cannot consume waste heat at all. Using it would defeat our own premise. MD is thermally driven, so it is the only mainstream option that actually eats the byproduct we claim to repurpose.
Isn't this the Oracle patent?That patent claims a hardware method — fixed topology, one steady liquid loop, seawater feed. It claims nothing about forecasting, dispatch or public verification. We're inland, mixed-cooling-type, and software-only.
How do you know MD rejects fluoride and nitrate?Thermally driven vapour–liquid separation excludes non-volatile ions by principle, and published MD fluoride-removal studies support it. Exact rejection is feed- and membrane-specific and needs facility validation — we say so rather than overclaim.
What happens to the concentrate?It is smaller and more concentrated than the original blowdown. Named pathway: ZLD crystalliser or licensed industrial reuse. It's on the schematic — we don't hide the residual stream.
Why should anyone trust your numbers?They shouldn't have to. Probes log automatically rather than being self-reported, and the chain is independently recomputable — press the audit button. That is the entire point of the product.
Where's the accountability today?Nowhere. No mandated public reporting exists, and no agency checks declared source against actual practice. That gap is the product.
Why software and not hardware?Heat availability tracks IT load, which is unpredictable across India's mixed fleet. A forecasting and dispatch layer is what makes any treatment hardware usable at all — and it works with what a facility already owns.
Who buys this?Operators, for compliance with the 2024 Liquid Waste Management Rules' 20%→50% reuse targets and for ESG reporting that survives scrutiny. And regulators — BWSSB, KSPCB, CGWB — who currently have no verification instrument at all.
Isn't seawater cooling the real answer?It isn't deployed anywhere in India yet, and the one flagship project's clearance doesn't even name a water source. Seawater can cut freshwater demand, but moving the problem from an aquifer to the ocean isn't a closed loop — there's still thermal discharge, intake entrainment and brine.
What if a facility just doesn't install your sensors?Then it's exactly where it is today — unverifiable. Our wedge is that the 2024 reuse mandates are arriving with real deadlines, and a facility that can prove its reuse fraction is in a materially better position than one that can only assert it.

What is real, what is simulated

Say this before anyone asks. Volunteering the boundary makes every other claim more credible.

Real

  • The physics: heat balance, loop ΔT, evaporation from latent heat, cycles of concentration, the Antoine vapour-pressure relationship, temperature polarisation, the flux model, and the concentration-then-rejection chain that produces permeate quality.
  • The regulatory framing: BIS IS 10500 limits, the Liquid Waste Management Rules 2024 targets, the CGWB extraction figures, the absence of any mandated reporting requirement.
  • The hash chain: genuinely computed and genuinely re-verifiable in the browser.
  • The data model: a real MongoDB schema you could deploy against.

Simulated

  • The telemetry — it's synthetic, generated by the model rather than read from a facility. The console says so in the footer.
  • The four facilities are composites built from published reporting, not live customers.
  • Coefficients (membrane permeability B, area, specific thermal energy) are typical literature values, not measurements from a specific membrane.
How to phrase it

"The plant is simulated, but it isn't faked — every number on screen is computed from a physical model, which is why changing a control on one page moves every other page consistently. What we'd need from a real deployment is calibration, not a redesign."
